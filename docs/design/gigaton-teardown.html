Reading the reference

The premium feeling is subtraction, not addition

The single most important finding, and the one that changes the plan: Gigaton's page is calmer than BlackGATE's. Its polish comes from a small number of devices executed with precision and repeated without variation — not from a rich ambient field.

Behind Gigaton's content there is grain, one very large soft plume of desaturated green–grey, a faint square grid in a few places, and a set of hairline arcs that connect sections. That is the entire background system. Nothing moves except on scroll.

BlackGATE's Ambience.tsx currently runs, simultaneously and permanently: three drifting aurora glows, a dot mesh on a 34-second loop, five travelling pulses on the column rules, two scrolling telemetry waves, a 17-second scan sweep, grain, and a vignette. It is more technically accomplished than anything on the reference site. It is also the reason the page reads busier and therefore cheaper.

So the work is asymmetric: take the four structural devices Gigaton has that BlackGATE lacks, and retire roughly half of the ambient layer that BlackGATE has and Gigaton deliberately does without.

The discipline worth stealing is not the hues — it is where they are allowed to appear. Those four chart hues occur only inside data panels. The chrome around them (nav, headings, cards, footer) is pure achromatic grey plus the one lime. Colour is a property of data, never of furniture.

BlackGATE already codifies this more strictly than Gigaton does — green means measured-now, blue means predicted — and that rule is written into global.css. Keep it. The gap is that BlackGATE's ambience currently leaks blue, violet and green into the background, which is exactly the furniture the rule says should stay grey.

Techniques

Nine devices, measured

01

Grain over everything

A fractal-noise layer sits above the gradients across the whole page. It does two jobs: it kills the banding that wide, low-contrast gradients produce on 8-bit panels, and it gives the black a physical tooth so it reads as a surface rather than as an empty buffer.

Opacity0.04 – 0.06
Tile140×140 SVG turbulence
Base frequency0.85, 3 octaves
Already have

Ambience.module.css ships this at 0.038. Raise slightly to 0.05 once the aurora is calmed — with less colour behind it, the tooth has to carry more.

02

One enormous desaturated plume

Behind the hero headline sits a single radial field, far larger than the viewport and barely coloured — a green-grey close to neutral, not a saturated glow. Because there is only one and it is almost achromatic, it reads as light in a room rather than as a gradient effect.

BlackGATE runs three saturated drifting glows (blue, violet, green). Collapse to one near-achromatic plume anchored behind the hero. This is the highest-impact single change on the page.

03

Hairline belt arcs as connective tissue

The most distinctive structural device on the reference site. Single-pixel arcs with a radius far larger than the viewport sweep between sections, passing behind panels and re-emerging below them. They imply one continuous curve running the length of the page, which is what makes separate sections feel like one machine.

In the closing section the same curve carries a solitary green dot sitting exactly on the path — an orbit marker. It is the only decorative use of the accent on the page and it lands because it is the only one.

Stroke1px, non-scaling
Colourrgba(255,255,255,0.10)
Radius2–4× viewport width
Gap — build it

BlackGATE has vertical column rules but nothing horizontal binding the sections. This is the one genuinely new structure to add, and it should replace the scrolling telemetry waves.

Belt arc · single accent marker on path
04

Mono micro-labels in low-alpha chips

The most-repeated device in the whole reference: every panel, chart, card and section is titled by a small uppercase monospace label sitting in a barely-there rounded chip. SELF LEARNING CONTROLLER PERFORMANCE. SPECIFIC FUEL COST ($/t). EXTRA PROFIT. REASON FOR AI ACTION | 15:40–16:10.

It works because it borrows the vernacular of instrumentation — these look like labels on a control panel, not like eyebrow text on a marketing page. The chip background is what separates it from an ordinary caption.

Radius5px
Partly have

BlackGATE uses bare mono eyebrows without the chip. Adding the chip is a two-line change that immediately buys most of the instrument-panel feel.

05

Weight inversion on the big numerals

A subtle move that does a lot of work. Section headings are set semibold to bold, but the large statistics — £1.3m, 3-5%, 10000t — are set light, around 250–300, at very large sizes.

The effect is that the numbers read as engraved measurements rather than as shouted marketing claims. Bold large numerals are what every SaaS template does; light large numerals are what instrument faces and technical drawings do.

06

Tick-mark bars instead of solid fills

In the explainability panel, attribution percentages are drawn not as filled bars but as a row of discrete 2px vertical ticks. A solid bar reads as a progress indicator; a row of ticks reads as a quantised instrument scale — an LED meter on a mixing desk.

It also solves a real problem: at small sizes a solid bar's rounded end caps make two similar values look identical. Discrete ticks stay countable.

Tick2px wide, 2px gap
Height11px
Colourseries hue, 0.9 alpha
Gap — adopt

Fits BlackGATE's existing green/blue split exactly: green ticks for measured contribution, blue for predicted. Use in the capabilities panel.

07

Registration marks on specimen cards

The materials cards (cement, steel, glass) carry a faint square grid and small coloured squares pinned at one corner with a matching dot at the opposite corner. They reference CAD registration marks and sample-annotation tags — the visual language of a materials lab rather than a product grid.

Each material gets its own mark colour, which is the only place the extended palette appears outside a chart.

Grid34px, alpha 0.05
Mark6px square, top-left
Dot7px circle, bottom-right
Gap — adopt

BlackGATE's Industries.tsx already frames five materials as specimens. The marks are a natural fit and give each material an identity without adding copy.

Plan

Order of work

Sequenced so the largest perceptual change lands first and each step is independently shippable.

01

Calm the ambient field

Three glows → one desaturated plume anchored to the hero. Remove waves, scan and four of five pulses. Freeze the mesh. This alone moves the page furthest toward the reference.

02

Add the belt arc layer

A single SVG layer of large-radius hairlines binding hero → stage → platform → results, with one green marker on the path in the closing section.

03

Chip every micro-label

One shared primitive, applied across every section eyebrow and panel title. Highest ratio of perceived polish to lines changed.

04

Invert numeral weight

Light display variant for statistics; headings stay semibold. Applies to Results and the hero figure.

05

Build the tick meter and registration marks

Tick meters into the capabilities panel using the green/blue split; per-material marks into the industries specimens.

06

Close the page

Frosted stat cards over the case-study photograph; oversized wordmark watermark in the footer.

Caveat

Where "better than them" actually comes from

Copying all nine devices would produce a competent Gigaton clone. The thing BlackGATE has that Gigaton does not is a colour rule with semantic meaning — green is measured, blue is predicted, everything else is grey — enforced in global.css and already respected across the console.

Gigaton's four chart hues are decorative; they distinguish series but carry no meaning you could state. BlackGATE's two carry a claim about the product: this number is real, that number is a forecast. Applied consistently to the tick meters, the belt marker, the prediction traces and the live pills, that reads as a system rather than a style — and a system is the thing that cannot be copied off a screenshot.

So the differentiator is not a new effect. It is applying the existing rule harder, while removing the ambient colour that currently violates it.
